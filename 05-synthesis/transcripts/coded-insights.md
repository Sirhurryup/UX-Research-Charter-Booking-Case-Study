## Purpose

This document summarizes early coded insights from nine user interviews about fishing charter booking behavior. The goal is to convert raw interview data into affinity map categories, themes, personas, and design recommendations.

---

## Early Research Summary

Across interviews, users described fishing charters as more than a simple booking transaction. They framed the experience around trust, clarity, comfort, social connection, and confidence that the trip would be worth the money.

Several participants emphasized that they need to understand what is included, who is leading the trip, how long the experience lasts, what they should bring, and whether the captain or crew can create a good experience.

## Sticky Notes for FigJam

### Motivations

- Wants to spend time with family or friends
- Sees fishing as relaxation
- Sees fishing as a new experience
- Wants a group or team bonding activity
- Wants to catch specific fish
- Wants to be outside near water
- Wants an experience that feels memorable

### Decision Drivers

- Availability matters when planning around visitors
- Price and promotions influence booking decisions
- Location and convenience influence selection
- The crew or captain can be more important than the location
- Clear package options make the decision easier
- Users want to know whether the experience fits their group
- Users compare schedule, cost, reviews, and overall fit
- Users want to know how far the boat goes out

### Trust Signals

- Reviews help users understand whether the experience is worth booking
- Testimonials from people who used the charter build confidence
- Social media photos help users judge real outcomes
- Captain knowledge is a major trust signal
- Crew friendliness influences confidence
- A personal phone call helps some users feel reassured
- A well-kept boat signals professionalism
- Known local reputation increases trust

### Information Needed

- How long does the trip last?
- What does the trip include?
- What should users bring?
- Are rods, reels, and bait provided?
- What kind of fish might users catch?
- Where will the charter go?
- What time of year is best?
- What are the weather or water conditions?
- What are the cost, duration, and package differences?
- What makes this experience different from another charter?

### Barriers and Uncertainty

- Users may hesitate if they cannot tell what is included
- Users may hesitate if package differences are unclear
- Users may need reassurance before booking for the first time
- Users may not know which charter to call without a referral
- Users may need social proof before trusting the provider
- Users may feel uncertain if the captain or crew are not visible
- Users may need more context than price alone

### Booking Preferences

- Some users are comfortable booking online
- Some users prefer to call before booking
- Some users want both options: online booking plus phone contact
- First-time users may want personal contact before committing
- Voice interaction can shape trust
- Clear booking options reduce friction

### Great Experience Drivers

- Catching fish matters
- A knowledgeable captain matters
- Friendly service matters
- Timely service matters
- The crew creates the tone of the experience
- The experience should feel enjoyable even if users do not catch much
- Seeing wildlife or being in nature adds value
- Food or drinks may make one charter stand out
- Users want the captain to create energy, not just operate the boat

### Repeat Booking Factors

- A good overall experience drives repeat booking
- Users want to recreate a good day with friends or family
- Captain knowledge can bring users back
- Friendly service can bring users back
- Catching fish increases the chance of return
- Feeling cared for increases the chance of return

## Early Themes

### Theme 1: Users need clarity before confidence

Users want clear information about what is included, how long the trip lasts, what they should bring, and what type of experience they are buying. When this information is visible, users feel more confident evaluating the value of a charter.

### Theme 2: Trust is built through people, not just pages

Participants repeatedly pointed to the captain, crew, testimonials, reviews, and phone conversations as key signals of trust. The human side of the service plays a major role in whether the experience feels safe, credible, and worth booking.

### Theme 3: Value is more than price

Price matters, but users also evaluate value through amenities, captain knowledge, catch potential, location, convenience, and the overall feeling that the trip will be enjoyable.

### Theme 4: The charter experience is social and emotional

Users described charters as family outings, group experiences, relaxation, bonding, and memory-making. The product is not only “fishing.” It is a guided experience that helps people connect, relax, and create a memorable day.

### Theme 5: Personal reassurance supports conversion

Several users were comfortable online, but still valued the ability to call, ask questions, and get a feel for the person behind the business. This suggests that online booking should not replace personal contact. It should support it.

---

## Early Design Implications

- Make the value proposition clear above the fold
- Show who the captain is and why users can trust him
- Add clear package cards with duration, price, inclusions, and ideal user type
- Explain what users should bring and what is provided
- Add testimonials and review highlights near decision points
- Include both “Book Now” and “Call with Questions” options
- Use photos that show the whole experience, not only fish
- Add content that explains locations, trip types, and what users can expect
- Position the charter as an experience, not just a service

---

## Preliminary Insight Statement

Fishing charter booking decisions are shaped by trust, clarity, and confidence. Users want to understand what they are buying, who is leading the experience, what is included, and whether the trip will feel worth the investment. A stronger digital experience should communicate the full value of the charter before asking users to book.
